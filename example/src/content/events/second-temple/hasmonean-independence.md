dating: |
  1 Maccabees says that "the yoke of the heathen" was taken from Israel in the 170th year of the Seleucid era and that documents were then dated by the first year of Simon [[bible:1 Macc 13:41-42]]. The era was counted from 312/311 BC, so the 170th year is 143/142 BC, and the grant of freedom from tribute is placed in 142 BC. Josephus gives the same figure of 170 years [[cite:simon]]. The citadel fell in the second month of year 171, the spring of 141 BC, and the assembly that confirmed Simon met in year 172, 140 BC [[bible:1 Macc 13:51]] [[bible:1 Macc 14:27]]. Simon was killed in the eleventh month of year 177, about February of 134 BC [[bible:1 Macc 16:14]].

  For Simon the source is 1 Maccabees, which is favourable to the family and is generally dated to the time of his son John Hyrcanus or soon after; Jonathan A. Goldstein, *I Maccabees* (Anchor Bible, 1976), puts it under Hyrcanus's son Alexander Jannaeus. The book is counted as canonical Scripture by the Catholic Church, as defined at Trent [[cite:trent]], and by the Orthodox Synod of Jerusalem of 1672 [[cite:dositheus]]. Protestant churches do not count it as canonical Scripture; the Church of England lists it among the books that are read "for example of life and instruction of manners" but not used to establish doctrine [[cite:articles]]. For John Hyrcanus the only narrative is that of Josephus, who wrote more than two hundred years later and drew on earlier historians such as Nicolaus of Damascus, whom he names [[cite:nicolaus]]. In the psalm quoted at the end, the rulers who usurped David's throne are not named; that they are the Hasmoneans is the usual reading (Kenneth Atkinson, *I Cried to the Lord*, 2004). Josephus gives Hyrcanus thirty-one years [[cite:kingship]], which puts his death in 104 BC.

[[person:judas-maccabeus]] died in battle, and his brother Jonathan took his place [[bible:1 Macc 9:18, 28-31]]. One of two rivals for the Seleucid throne appointed him high priest, and he first officiated at the feast of Booths in the 160th year, 152 BC [[bible:1 Macc 10:1-2, 15-21]]. He was later seized by treachery and killed by the usurper Tryphon [[bible:1 Macc 12:46-48]] [[bible:1 Macc 13:23]]. The people then acclaimed the last of the brothers, [[person:simon-thassi]], as their leader [[bible:1 Macc 13:1-9]].

Simon took the side of King Demetrius II against Tryphon, and Demetrius in return cancelled the tribute owed by the Jews. In the 170th year, says 1 Maccabees, "was the yoke of the heathen taken away from Israel", and the people began to date their contracts by the years of Simon, "the great high priest and captain and leader of the Jews" [[bible:1 Macc 13:34-42]]. The next year the Seleucid garrison that had held the citadel in [[place:jerusalem]] since the days of [[person:antiochus-iv|Antiochus IV]] was starved out. Simon entered it with palm branches, harps and hymns [[bible:1 Macc 13:49-52]].

In the third year a great assembly of priests, people and elders recorded its decision on bronze tablets set up on Mount Zion. Simon was to be their leader and high priest "forever, until there should arise a faithful prophet", to command the army and to wear purple and gold [[bible:1 Macc 14:27-47]]. [[place:rome]] renewed the friendship it had made with his brothers [[bible:1 Macc 14:16-19]].

Simon was murdered at a banquet near [[place:jericho]] by his son-in-law, and his son [[person:john-hyrcanus]] succeeded him as high priest [[bible:1 Macc 16:11-24]]. There 1 Maccabees ends, and [[person:josephus]] becomes the only narrative. He reports that Antiochus VII besieged Jerusalem and that Hyrcanus bought peace [[cite:siege]]. After that king died, Hyrcanus took [[place:shechem]] and Mount Gerizim, where the Samaritan temple stood, and conquered the Idumeans to the south, who might stay if they accepted circumcision and the Jewish law [[cite:conquests]]. Josephus also tells how Hyrcanus, once a disciple of the Pharisees, broke with them at the urging of a Sadducee friend [[cite:pharisees]]. On his death, Josephus says, his son Aristobulus was the first of the family to put on a crown [[cite:kingship]].

The Hasmoneans thus joined the high priesthood to political rule and then to kingship, though they were a priestly family and not of David's line [[bible:1 Macc 2:1]]. A psalm written in Jerusalem after the Roman conquest accuses rulers who "laid waste the throne of David" and prays for a king who is David's son [[cite:psalm]]. The Pharisees and Sadducees whom the Gospels show disputing with [[person:jesus]] appear as rival parties in Josephus's account of these years [[cite:pharisees]] [[bible:Matt 22:23, 34]]. And the Idumeans whom Hyrcanus brought into the Jewish people were the nation of Antipater, the father of [[person:herod-the-great|Herod]] [[cite:antipater]]. Independence ended when Rome stepped into a quarrel between two brothers of the family; see Pompey Takes Jerusalem.
