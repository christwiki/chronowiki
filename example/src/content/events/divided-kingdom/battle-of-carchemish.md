In 609 BC Pharaoh [[person:necho-ii]] marched north to the Euphrates. King Josiah of Judah went out against him at Megiddo and was killed [[bible:2 Kings 23:29-30]]. The Babylonian chronicle for that year records a large Egyptian army crossing the Euphrates with the last king of Assyria to attack [[place:haran|Harran]], without success [[cite:harran]]. Necho then removed Josiah's son Jehoahaz after a reign of three months and took him to Egypt. He put another son, Eliakim, on the throne under the name [[person:jehoiakim]], and laid on Judah a tribute of a hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold [[bible:2 Kings 23:31-35]].

Four years later the Egyptian army was at [[place:carchemish]] on the Euphrates. The Babylonian chronicle tells what happened in a few lines. In the twenty-first year of Nabopolassar the king stayed at home, and his eldest son, the crown prince [[person:nebuchadnezzar-ii]], led the army to Carchemish, crossed the river and attacked. The Egyptians gave way and were beaten. Those who got away were overtaken in the district of Hamath, and the chronicle says that not one man escaped to his own country. Nebuchadnezzar then conquered the whole region of Hamath [[cite:chronicle]].

Jeremiah dates the same battle to the fourth year of Jehoiakim and names the pharaoh: it was the army of Necho that Nebuchadnezzar struck at Carchemish. His poem on the defeat says of the Egyptian warriors, "In the north by the river Euphrates they have stumbled and fallen" [[bible:Jer 46:2-6]].

Nabopolassar died that summer. Nebuchadnezzar returned to [[place:babylon]] and took the throne [[cite:accession]]. In the rest of that year and the next he marched unopposed through what the chronicle calls the Hatti-land, that is, Syria and Palestine, and its kings came before him with tribute [[cite:tribute]]. Kings sums up the outcome: the king of Egypt did not come out of his land again, because the king of Babylon had taken everything that had been his from the Brook of Egypt to the Euphrates [[bible:2 Kings 24:7]].

For Judah the battle meant a change of master. Jehoiakim served Nebuchadnezzar for three years and then rebelled [[bible:2 Kings 24:1]], which led to [[event:first-deportation-to-babylon]]. In that same fourth year of Jehoiakim, Jeremiah announced that Judah and the nations around it would serve the king of Babylon for seventy years [[bible:Jer 25:1]] [[bible:Jer 25:8-11]].
