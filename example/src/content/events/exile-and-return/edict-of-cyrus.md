When [[person:cyrus-ii]] took [[place:babylon]] in 539 BC, the exiles from Judah passed from Babylonian to Persian rule.

The book of Ezra opens with a proclamation issued in Cyrus's first year. The God of heaven, it says, has charged him to build a house in [[place:jerusalem]]; any of that God's people may go up to build it, and their neighbours are to support them with silver, gold, goods and animals [[bible:Ezra 1:1-4]]. Ezra gives a second version in Aramaic, a memorandum said to have been found in the royal archives at Ecbatana, which sets the dimensions of the building, charges the cost to the royal treasury and orders the return of the gold and silver vessels that Nebuchadnezzar had taken [[bible:Ezra 6:1-5]]. The books of Chronicles end with the same decree [[bible:2 Chr 36:22-23]].

The temple vessels were counted out to [[person:sheshbazzar]], called the prince of Judah, who brought them to Jerusalem and, according to a later report, laid the temple's foundations [[bible:Ezra 1:7-11]] [[bible:Ezra 5:14-16]]. The list of those who returned is headed by [[person:zerubbabel]], a grandson of King Jehoiachin [[bible:1 Chr 3:17-19]], and by the priest [[person:jeshua-the-high-priest]]; it totals 42,360 people [[bible:Ezra 2:1-2, 64]]. They rebuilt the altar and began the temple, and then the work stalled for years [[bible:Ezra 3:1-4:5]].

The Cyrus Cylinder, written in Babylon shortly after the conquest, records measures of the same kind. In it Cyrus says that he sent the images of gods back to their sanctuaries in a list of cities beyond the Tigris, and that he gathered their people and returned them to their settlements [[cite:cylinder]]. The cylinder does not mention Judah or Jerusalem. What it shows is that Cyrus claimed credit for sending displaced gods and their peoples home elsewhere in his realm, which is the kind of act Ezra describes.

Isaiah 40–55 names Cyrus as the Lord's shepherd and anointed, through whom Jerusalem and its temple would be rebuilt [[bible:Isa 44:28-45:4]]. Ezra presents the decree as the fulfilment of Jeremiah's word that the exile would end [[bible:Ezra 1:1; Jer 29:10]].
