After his victory at Carchemish, [[person:nebuchadnezzar-ii]] made Judah a vassal of Babylon. King Jehoiakim rebelled, and Kings records his death before it describes the siege [[bible:2 Kings 24:1-6, 10]]. His son [[person:jehoiachin]], eighteen years old, reigned for three months, and in that time [[place:jerusalem]] was besieged [[bible:2 Kings 24:8-11]].

Jehoiachin surrendered with his mother, his officials and his court. Nebuchadnezzar carried off the treasures of the temple and the palace and deported the king, the leading families, the soldiers and the craftsmen to [[place:babylon]]. He made Jehoiachin's uncle Mattaniah king and renamed him [[person:zedekiah]] [[bible:2 Kings 24:12-17]]. Kings speaks of ten thousand captives; a list preserved in Jeremiah counts 3,023 [[bible:Jer 52:28]].

Babylon's court chronicle records the same event from the other side. In his seventh year, it says, the king marched west, besieged "the city of Judah", took it on the second day of Addaru, captured its king, appointed a king of his own choosing and carried heavy tribute back to Babylon [[cite:chronicle]].

Among the deported was the priest [[person:ezekiel]], who dates his visions from the year of Jehoiachin's exile [[bible:Ezek 1:1-3]]. Jehoiachin himself appears in administrative tablets excavated at Babylon, which list oil rations issued to the king of Judah and his retinue [[cite:rations]]. The books of Kings end with his release from prison thirty-seven years later [[bible:2 Kings 25:27-30]].

The deportation removed Judah's king and much of its leadership a decade before the city itself was destroyed. Jeremiah wrote to the exiles, telling them to build houses, raise families and seek the welfare of the city where they now lived [[bible:Jer 29:1-14]].
