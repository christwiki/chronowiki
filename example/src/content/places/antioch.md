[[person:josephus|Josephus]] calls Antioch the metropolis of Syria and the third city of the Roman world, and says that it had a large Jewish community [[cite:third-city]] [[cite:jews]]. Acts says that believers scattered from [[place:jerusalem|Jerusalem]] preached there to non-Jews, Barnabas and Paul taught there, and "the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch" [[bible:Acts 11:19-26]]; see the Church at Antioch, Where Believers Were First Called Christians. The church sent the two out on the first missionary journey [[bible:Acts 13:1-3]]. The dispute that led to the the Council of Jerusalem began there, and there Paul confronted Peter [[bible:Acts 15:1-2]] [[bible:Gal 2:11-14]]. Ignatius of Antioch calls himself "the bishop of Syria" in his letter to the Romans [[cite:ignatius]]. It is to be distinguished from Pisidian Antioch in Asia Minor.
