Megiddo is the mound of Tel Megiddo (Tell el-Mutesellim) in northern Israel, about thirty kilometres south-east of Haifa. Joshua counts its king among those defeated by Israel, and Judges says that the tribe of Manasseh did not drive out its people [[bible:Josh 12:7, 21]] [[bible:Judg 1:27]]. The Song of Deborah sets the battle with the kings of Canaan "by the waters of Megiddo" [[bible:Judg 5:19]]. Kings names it with Hazor and Gezer among the places for which Solomon raised forced labour [[bible:1 Kings 9:15]]. A fragment of a monument of Pharaoh Shoshenq I was found on the site [[cite:shoshenq]].

Kings says that Ahaziah of Judah, struck down on the orders of Jehu, fled to Megiddo and died there [[bible:2 Kings 9:27]], and that Pharaoh [[person:necho-ii]] killed [[person:josiah]] there [[bible:2 Kings 23:29-30]]. Chronicles has Josiah wounded by archers in the plain of Megiddo and brought to Jerusalem [[bible:2 Chr 35:22-24]]; see [[event:death-of-josiah|Josiah falls at Megiddo]]. Zechariah compares a mourning to come in Jerusalem with a mourning in the plain of Megiddo [[bible:Zech 12:11]].
