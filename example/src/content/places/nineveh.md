Nineveh stood on the east bank of the Tigris; its mounds now lie within the city of Mosul. Genesis counts it among the cities of Assyria founded in the days of Nimrod [[bible:Gen 10:8-12]]. [[person:sennacherib]] calls it his capital [[cite:capital]]. Kings says he returned there after his campaign against Judah [[bible:2 Kings 19:36]], and the [[source:lachish-reliefs]] come from his palace in the city. The book of Jonah calls it "that great city" and tells of its repentance [[bible:Jonah 1:2]] [[bible:Jonah 3:5-10]]; Nahum and Zephaniah announce its destruction [[bible:Nah 3:1-7]] [[bible:Zeph 2:13-15]]. The Babylonian chronicle records that Babylonian and Median armies besieged the city for three months in 612 BC, plundered it and left it a ruin [[cite:fall]]. Jesus named its people as an example of repentance [[bible:Matt 12:41]].
