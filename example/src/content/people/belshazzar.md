Babylonian texts know Belshazzar as the eldest son of [[person:nabonidus]], who prays for him in a building inscription from Ur [[cite:ur-cylinder]]. While his father lived at Tayma, the chronicle places "the prince" with the officers and the army in Babylonia [[cite:chronicle]], and a poem hostile to Nabonidus says that the king entrusted the army and the kingship to his first-born [[cite:verse]]. These texts call him the king's son. None of them gives him the title of king.

The book of Daniel calls him king and speaks of Nebuchadnezzar as his father. At his feast the vessels of the Jerusalem temple are brought out, a hand writes on the wall, and he is killed the same night [[bible:Dan 5:1-30]]. Daniel also dates two visions by the years of his reign [[bible:Dan 7:1]] [[bible:Dan 8:1]]. The cuneiform sources do not say how he died.
