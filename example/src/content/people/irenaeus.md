Irenaeus says that in his early youth he saw Polycarp, and in a letter that Eusebius quotes he recalls the place where the old bishop sat and taught "in lower Asia" [[cite:polycarp]] [[cite:florinus]]. By 177 he was a presbyter at Lyon. The imprisoned Christians of that city sent him to Rome with a letter commending him to bishop Eleutherus [[cite:presbyter]], and after the death of Pothinus he became bishop [[cite:bishop]].

He wrote *Against Heresies* while Eleutherius was bishop of Rome [[cite:eleutherius]]. When Victor of Rome, about 190, moved to break communion with the churches of Asia over the date of Easter, Irenaeus wrote to dissuade him, reminding him that Polycarp and Anicetus had differed on the same point and remained at peace; Eusebius remarks that he lived up to his name, which means "peaceful" [[cite:victor]]. See Irenaeus Sets Out the Rule of Faith and the Martyrs of Lyon and Vienne.
