Nabonidus came to the throne of [[place:babylon]] through a conspiracy, according to the Babylonian historian Berossus, and reigned seventeen years [[cite:berossus]]. For much of that time he lived at Tayma in Arabia while the crown prince, [[person:belshazzar]], stayed in Babylonia with the army [[cite:chronicle]]. A hostile poem written after his fall says that he entrusted the kingship to his eldest son when he left [[cite:verse]], and the inscription of [[person:cyrus-ii]] accuses him of neglecting Marduk, the city's god [[cite:cylinder]]. In 539 BC his army was beaten at Opis; he fled and was captured [[cite:chronicle]]. Berossus says that Cyrus sent him to live in Carmania [[cite:berossus]].

The Bible does not mention him. A Jewish text found at Qumran tells of his seven-year affliction at Tayma and of a Jewish exorcist who pardoned his sins, a story close to the one that Daniel 4 tells of Nebuchadnezzar [[cite:prayer]].
