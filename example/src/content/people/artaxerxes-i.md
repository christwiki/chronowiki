In the book of Ezra a king Artaxerxes receives a complaint from officials in Samaria that Jerusalem is being refortified, and orders the work stopped [[bible:Ezra 4:7-23]]. An Artaxerxes sends [[person:ezra]] to Jerusalem in his seventh year [[bible:Ezra 7:7, 11-13]], and in his twentieth year an Artaxerxes sends his cupbearer [[person:nehemiah]] to rebuild the city [[bible:Neh 2:1-8]]. A papyrus of 407 BC, which names the next generation of Nehemiah's opponents and a later high priest, shows that Nehemiah's king was the first of the three Persian kings called Artaxerxes [[cite:elephantine]]. Whether Ezra's king was the same man is debated; see [[event:ezra-and-the-law]].

Business tablets from Nippur are dated by the years of his reign [[cite:murashu]]. Josephus calls Nehemiah's king Xerxes [[cite:josephus-nehemiah]] and gives the name Artaxerxes, son of Xerxes, to the king of the book of Esther [[cite:josephus-esther]].
