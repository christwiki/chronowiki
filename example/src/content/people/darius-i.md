Darius, son of Hystaspes, was not a son of [[person:cyrus-ii]]. By his own account he killed a usurper in the autumn of 522 BC, took the kingship and spent the next year putting down revolts [[cite:behistun]]. Herodotus says that he reigned thirty-six years and died while preparing campaigns against Egypt and Athens, leaving the throne to his son Xerxes [[cite:herodotus]].

The prophets [[person:haggai]] and [[person:zechariah]] date their messages by his years [[bible:Hag 1:1]] [[bible:Zech 1:1]]. According to Ezra, when his governor questioned the rebuilding of the temple in Jerusalem, Darius had the archives searched, found Cyrus's decree and ordered the work to be finished at royal expense [[bible:Ezra 6:1-12]]. The book of Daniel also has a "Darius the Mede", who receives the kingdom of Babylon and is named before Cyrus [[bible:Dan 5:31; 6:28]]. No source independent of Daniel knows such a king.
