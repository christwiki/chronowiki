---
name: Isaiah
altNames: [Esaias]
role: Prophet in Jerusalem, c. 740–700 BC
summary: Prophet of Judah under Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah. He called Jerusalem's kings to trust God when Assyria threatened, and the New Testament quotes his book for the birth, ministry and suffering of Jesus.
---

Isaiah son of Amoz prophesied in [[place:jerusalem]] under four kings of Judah, from Uzziah to [[person:hezekiah]] [[bible:Isa 1:1]]. He dates the vision in which he was sent to the year Uzziah died [[bible:Isa 6:1-8]]. He confronted Ahaz during the attack by Syria and Israel [[bible:Isa 7:1-17]], went naked and barefoot as a sign in the year the Assyrians took Ashdod [[bible:Isa 20:1-2]], and told Hezekiah that [[person:sennacherib]] would not enter the city [[bible:Isa 37:21-35]]. Chronicles names him as a writer of the acts of Uzziah and of Hezekiah [[bible:2 Chr 26:22]] [[bible:2 Chr 32:32]].

The later chapters of the book speak to the exiles in Babylon and name Cyrus [[bible:Isa 44:28-45:1]]; see [[event:prophecies-of-return]]. In Luke, Jesus reads from the scroll of Isaiah in the synagogue at Nazareth [[bible:Luke 4:16-21]], and in Acts Philip explains a passage of Isaiah to an Ethiopian official as speaking of Jesus [[bible:Acts 8:27-35]].
