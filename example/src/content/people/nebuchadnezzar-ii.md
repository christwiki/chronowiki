As crown prince, Nebuchadnezzar defeated the Egyptian army at Carchemish in 605 BC and succeeded his father Nabopolassar the same year [[cite:chronicle]]. The same chronicle shows him campaigning in the west in almost every year down to 594 BC, where the tablet ends [[cite:campaigns]]. The Babylonian historian Berossus, as quoted by Josephus, gives him a reign of forty-three years [[cite:berossus]], and the book of Daniel has him speak of "this great Babylon, which I have built" [[bible:Dan 4:30]].

In the Bible he is the king who ends the kingdom of Judah [[bible:2 Kings 24:10-17]] [[bible:2 Kings 25:1-21]]. Jeremiah calls him the Lord's servant, the instrument of judgment on Jerusalem [[bible:Jer 25:9]], and the stories of Daniel are set at his court [[bible:Dan 1:1-7]].
