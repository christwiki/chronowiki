In 609 BC an Egyptian army crossed the Euphrates in support of the last king of Assyria [[cite:harran]]. Kings says that Pharaoh Necho was on his way there when King [[person:josiah]] went out against him at Megiddo and was killed [[bible:2 Kings 23:29]]; Chronicles has Necho warn Josiah first that his quarrel was not with Judah [[bible:2 Chr 35:20-22]]. Necho then deposed Josiah's son Jehoahaz, made [[person:jehoiakim]] king and put Judah under tribute [[bible:2 Kings 23:33-35]]. In 605 BC the Babylonian crown prince [[person:nebuchadnezzar-ii]] destroyed the Egyptian army at [[place:carchemish]] [[cite:chronicle]] [[bible:Jer 46:2]], and Egypt lost everything it had held between the Brook of Egypt and the Euphrates [[bible:2 Kings 24:7]].

Herodotus, writing about 170 years later, says that Necos left off digging a canal, built warships, defeated the Syrians at Magdolus, took the city of Cadytis and reigned sixteen years [[cite:herodotus]].
