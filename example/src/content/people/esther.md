role: Jewish queen of Persia in the book of Esther
summary: The Jewish orphan who, in the book that bears her name, becomes queen of Persia and risks her life to save her people from destruction. The feast of Purim recalls the story.
---

Esther, whose Hebrew name was Hadassah, was the daughter of Abihail and was brought up by her cousin [[person:mordecai]] after her parents died [[bible:Esth 2:7, 15]]. The book says that King Ahasuerus made her queen in his seventh year [[bible:Esth 2:16-17]]. When a decree went out to kill the Jews of the empire she went to the king unsummoned, at the risk of her life, exposed its author Haman, and obtained a second decree that let the Jews defend themselves [[bible:Esth 4:15-5:2]] [[bible:Esth 7:3-6]] [[bible:Esth 8:3-11]]. With Mordecai she established the feast of Purim [[bible:Esth 9:29-32]].

No source independent of the book mentions her. On the question of its historical basis see [[event:esther-and-purim]].
