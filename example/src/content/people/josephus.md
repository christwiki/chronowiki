Josephus, son of Matthias, was born in the first year of the emperor Gaius into a priestly family of [[place:jerusalem]], and says that as a young man he tried the Jewish schools in turn and chose to follow the Pharisees [[cite:family]]. When the revolt broke out he was put in charge of Galilee [[cite:galilee]]. He was captured when Jotapata fell in the thirteenth year of Nero, AD 67 [[cite:taken]], and, by his own account, told Vespasian that he would become emperor [[cite:jotapata]].

He was later freed, was with Titus at the siege of Jerusalem, and afterwards lived at Rome with citizenship, a pension and lodging in a house that had been Vespasian's [[cite:rome]]. There he wrote the *Jewish War*, which he says he presented to Vespasian and Titus [[cite:presented]], and the *Jewish Antiquities*, finished in the thirteenth year of Domitian, AD 93–94, in the fifty-sixth year of his life [[cite:finished]]. The works cited here do not give the year of his death.

He writes as a participant with a case to make, about himself and about who was to blame for the war. His books are also a detailed account of Judaea in the first century, and he mentions James the Just as the brother of "Jesus, who was called Christ" [[cite:james]].
