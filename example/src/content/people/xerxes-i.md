Xerxes calls himself son of King Darius and lists the lands that paid him tribute, from India to Nubia [[cite:xph]]. Herodotus says that he succeeded [[person:darius-i]], put down a revolt in Egypt, spent four years preparing and in the fifth led a vast army against Greece [[cite:accession]]. After his defeat there he returned to [[place:susa]], where Herodotus sets a story of intrigue involving his wife Amestris [[cite:amestris]].

The Bible names a King Ahasuerus between Darius and Artaxerxes [[bible:Ezra 4:5-7]], and the book of Esther is set at his court in Susa [[bible:Esth 1:1-3]]. Ahasuerus is the Hebrew form of the name that the Greeks wrote as Xerxes. The ancient Greek version of Esther calls the king Artaxerxes; see [[event:esther-and-purim]].
