Sennacherib ruled from [[place:nineveh]]. His annals describe the invasion of Judah as his third campaign: forty-six towns taken, [[person:hezekiah]] shut up in [[place:jerusalem]], and a heavy tribute sent after him to Nineveh [[cite:prism]]. Reliefs from his palace show the capture of [[place:lachish]] [[cite:relief]]. Kings tells of his threats against Jerusalem, the loss of his army in a night and his return home [[bible:2 Kings 18:13-19:36]].

The Babylonian chronicle records that he was killed by his son in a rebellion on the twentieth of the month Tebetu, after a reign of twenty-four years, and that his son Esarhaddon took the throne about two months later [[cite:death]]. Kings and Isaiah name two sons, Adrammelech and Sharezer, as the killers [[bible:2 Kings 19:37]] [[bible:Isa 37:38]].
