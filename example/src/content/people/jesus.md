Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee [[bible:Mark 1:9]]. Matthew and Luke tell of his birth at Bethlehem, in stories set in the days of Herod [[bible:Matt 2:1]] [[bible:Luke 1:5; 2:4-7]], and Luke says that he was about thirty when he began to teach [[bible:Luke 3:23]]. After his baptism by John the Baptist, the Gospels relate, he went through Galilee announcing that the kingdom of God was at hand [[bible:Mark 1:9, 14-15]], taught, healed, and gathered disciples, twelve of whom he chose as his closest companions [[bible:Mark 3:13-19]]. He was crucified at [[place:jerusalem]] on the order of the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, under the charge "The King of the Jews" [[bible:Mark 15:15, 24-26]]. His followers proclaimed that God had raised him from the dead; an early written form of that proclamation, which Paul says he had himself received, stands in his first letter to the Corinthians [[bible:1 Cor 15:3-8]]. They confessed him as the Christ, that is the Messiah, as Lord and as Son of God [[bible:Mark 8:29]] [[bible:Rom 1:3-4]] [[bible:Phil 2:11]].

The four Gospels are the main sources for his life; see the Four Gospels Are Written. Writers from outside the Christian movement also mention him. The Roman historian Tacitus, about AD 115, says that "Christus" was put to death by Pontius Pilate in the reign of Tiberius [[cite:tacitus]]. The Jewish historian [[person:josephus]], in AD 93–94, identifies a man put to death in Jerusalem some thirty years later as James, "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ" [[cite:james]]. Josephus also has a paragraph on Jesus himself; in the form in which it survives it calls him the Christ and speaks of his resurrection, and most scholars think that Christian copyists altered what Josephus wrote [[cite:josephus]]. The dates of his birth and death are discussed under the Birth of Jesus and the Crucifixion.
