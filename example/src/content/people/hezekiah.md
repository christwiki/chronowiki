Hezekiah son of Ahaz became king at twenty-five and reigned twenty-nine years [[bible:2 Kings 18:1-2]]. He removed the high places and destroyed the bronze serpent that Moses had made, and Kings says that no king of Judah trusted the Lord as he did [[bible:2 Kings 18:3-6]]. Chronicles describes his reopening of the temple and a Passover to which he invited the northern tribes [[bible:2 Chr 29:3]] [[bible:2 Chr 30:1-5]]. He rebelled against Assyria and brought water into [[place:jerusalem]] by a conduit [[bible:2 Kings 18:7]] [[bible:2 Kings 20:20]]. When [[person:sennacherib]] invaded he paid tribute but kept the city [[bible:2 Kings 18:13-16]] [[bible:2 Kings 19:35-36]]; Sennacherib's annals name him and list the tribute [[cite:prism]]. For the difficulty of dating his reign see [[event:hezekiah-and-sennacherib]].

Kings also tells of his illness and recovery, and of the envoys from Babylon to whom he showed his treasures, after which [[person:isaiah]] foretold that all of it would be carried to Babylon [[bible:2 Kings 20:1-19]]. A collection of proverbs is credited to his scribes [[bible:Prov 25:1]], and he stands in Matthew's genealogy of Jesus [[bible:Matt 1:9-10]].
