Ptolemy II, called Philadelphus, was the son of Ptolemy son of Lagus, the general of [[person:alexander-the-great]] who made himself king of Egypt. Josephus gives the father a reign of forty years and the son one of thirty-nine [[cite:reign]].

The Letter of Aristeas sets his court at [[place:alexandria]] and presents him as a collector of books who asked the high priest in Jerusalem for scholars to translate the Jewish law, and who received them with honour [[cite:aristeas]]. It also says that he freed the Jews whom his father had brought to Egypt as slaves [[cite:freed]]. Philo, three centuries later, calls him the most illustrious of the Ptolemies [[cite:philo]]. These are Jewish traditions about him, written a century and more after his death. See [[event:septuagint]].
