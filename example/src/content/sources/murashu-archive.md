In May 1893 the University of Pennsylvania's expedition to Nippur found a room containing about 730 clay tablets, the records of a firm that its editor called "Murashû Sons". The tablets are dated by the regnal years of the Persian kings Artaxerxes I and Darius II. They record loans, leases and deliveries in and around [[place:nippur]].

Tablet no. 4 of the first published volume, dated to the twenty-second year of Artaxerxes, provides for a delivery of dates "at the canal Kabaru". The editor, Hermann Hilprecht, identified this canal with the river Chebar of [[event:ezekiel-among-the-exiles]], and he noted how many of the people named in the tablets bear names known from Ezra and Nehemiah. The tablets are evidence for where descendants of the exiles lived a century and a half after the deportations. They are not from Ezekiel's own time.
