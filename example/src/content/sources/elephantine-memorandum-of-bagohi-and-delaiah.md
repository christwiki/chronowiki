The papyrus is complete. It is not a letter with an address and a date but a memorandum: a record, in the first person, of what Bagohi and Delaiah "said to me", for use before Arsames in Egypt. The museum's record dates it between 407 and 400 BC. Cowley took it for the answer to the petition of November 407 BC ([[source:elephantine-petition-to-bagohi]]), which was addressed to Bagohi as governor of Judah and says that the same request had gone to Delaiah and Shelemiah, the sons of Sanballat, governor of Samaria.

The message is that the "altar-house of the God of Heaven" at [[place:elephantine]], built before Cambyses and demolished by Vidranga in the fourteenth year of King Darius, is to be rebuilt on its site as it was, and that meal-offering and incense are to be offered on its altar as before. The petition had asked for meal-offering, incense and burnt offering. See [[event:elephantine-temple]].
