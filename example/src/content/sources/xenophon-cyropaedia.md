Xenophon of Athens marched into the Persian empire with a Greek mercenary army in 401 BC and later wrote this portrait of [[person:cyrus-ii]] as the model ruler. It was composed about 170 years after Cyrus took Babylon, and much of it is invention in the service of its argument.

Its account of the capture of the city agrees with Herodotus that the Persians entered along the bed of the Euphrates during a night festival, and adds that the Babylonian king was killed in his palace. It does not name the king. See [[event:fall-of-babylon]].
