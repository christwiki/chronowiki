The Babylonian chronicles are terse year-by-year records, written in Akkadian on clay tablets, of what happened to the kings of Babylon and their neighbours. Chronicle 1 in A. K. Grayson's edition (ABC 1) runs from the Babylonian king Nabonassar to the accession of Shamash-shuma-ukin. It survives in two copies in the British Museum. The better preserved is a large tablet with two columns on each side. Its colophon says that it was written at Babylon in the twenty-second year of Darius I, which is 500/499 BC (iv.39–43).

For the history of Israel and Judah three entries matter. The chronicle says that [[person:shalmaneser-v]] "ravaged Samaria" (i.27–28), which is the only event it records for his reign and the Babylonian counterpart to [[event:fall-of-samaria]]. It dates the accession of [[person:sargon-ii]] to the twelfth day of the month Tebetu (i.31). And it reports that [[person:sennacherib]] was killed by his son in a rebellion on the twentieth of Tebetu (iii.34–35), a death that Kings and Isaiah also report, naming two sons [[bible:2 Kings 19:37]] [[bible:Isa 37:38]]. The chronicle is written from Babylon's point of view and has no stake in either the Assyrian or the biblical telling.
