Josephus wrote the *War* at Rome in the 70s. He says in the preface that he first composed it in the language of his own country and then put it into Greek. The seven books begin with the seizure of Jerusalem by Antiochus Epiphanes in the second century BC and end with the fall of Masada, but most of the work concerns the revolt that broke out in AD 66.

He took part in that war on both sides: as commander in Galilee until his surrender in 67, then in the Roman camp, where he was present at the siege of Jerusalem in 70. That makes him an eyewitness of much that he reports. It also shapes the report. He wrote under the patronage of Vespasian and Titus, he lays the blame for the disaster on the leaders of the Jewish rebel factions, and he says that Titus tried to save the Temple from the fire (6.254–266). His figures for armies and for the dead are widely thought to be too high.
