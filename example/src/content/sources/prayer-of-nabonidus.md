The fragments come from Cave 4 at Qumran and preserve the opening of a first-person account by [[person:nabonidus]], the last king of Babylon. He says that he was struck with a disease for seven years while he was at Tayma, lived apart from other people, and was told by a Jewish exile to write his story down to the honour of the Most High God. The text breaks off as he describes praying to gods of silver, gold, bronze, iron, wood and stone.

The copy was made some five centuries after Nabonidus, and it is a Jewish story, not a Babylonian record. It matters for [[event:daniel-in-babylon]] because Daniel 4 tells a similar story of Nebuchadnezzar, while Babylonian sources place Nabonidus, not Nebuchadnezzar, away from his capital for years. Much of the published text is restored from small fragments.
