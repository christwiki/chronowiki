The Septuagint is the Greek version of Israel's Scriptures. The five books of Moses were translated first, by tradition at Alexandria under Ptolemy II, and the remaining books followed over the next two centuries. The New Testament writers usually quote the Old Testament in this form, and it remains the Old Testament of the Greek Orthodox Church.

Where it differs from the Hebrew text that underlies most modern translations, it is a witness to how the text was read, or to a different Hebrew wording, before the time of Christ. Two differences matter for chronology: the ages in the genealogies of Genesis 5 and 11 are mostly higher, and at Exodus 12:40 the 430 years cover the time in Canaan as well as in Egypt.
