The text is one of the standard inscriptions of [[person:xerxes-i]], cut in Old Persian with Elamite and Babylonian versions. After praising the god Ahuramazda, the king names himself son of Darius and lists some thirty lands that paid him tribute, among them Babylonia, Egypt, the Greeks on both sides of the sea, India and the Nubians. He then says that he put down a rebellion and destroyed a sanctuary of false gods, the *daiva* from which the inscription takes its modern name.

It is the king's own statement of the extent of his empire. The book of Esther describes the realm of Ahasuerus in the same terms, as reaching "from India even to Ethiopia"; see [[event:esther-and-purim]].
