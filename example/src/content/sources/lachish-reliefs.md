The panels are gypsum wall slabs from Room XXXVI of the South-West Palace of [[person:sennacherib]] at [[place:nineveh]], carved in low relief in the years after the campaign of 701 BC. They show the assault on a fortified city and its aftermath. On one panel the king sits on a throne before his tent while prisoners are brought to him. A cuneiform caption of four lines over the scene says that Sennacherib, king of the world, king of Assyria, sat on his throne while the spoil of [[place:lachish]] passed before him.

The reliefs are the Assyrian court's own picture of an event that the Bible mentions only in passing [[bible:2 Kings 18:14, 17]] [[bible:2 Chr 32:9]]. Sennacherib's written annals do not name Lachish; the caption does. See [[event:hezekiah-and-sennacherib]].
