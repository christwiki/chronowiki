The tablet is a piece of polemic composed in Babylon under Persian rule. It accuses [[person:nabonidus]] of neglecting Marduk for the moon god, of stopping the New Year festival and of leaving the country for Tayma in Arabia, and it contrasts him with the pious [[person:cyrus-ii]].

For all its bias it is close to the events. Its statement that Nabonidus "entrusted the kingship" to his first-born son when he left for Tayma is the nearest that any Babylonian text comes to the title that the book of Daniel gives to [[person:belshazzar]].
