Philo was a Jewish philosopher of [[place:alexandria]] who lived from about 20 BC to about AD 50. His two books on Moses present the lawgiver to readers who knew Greek philosophy better than the Scriptures.

In the second book he tells how Ptolemy Philadelphus sent to the high priest for translators, how they withdrew to the island of Pharos, and how they all arrived at the same wording, "as if some unseen prompter had suggested all their language to them". He adds that in his own day Jews and others crossed to the island every year to keep a feast in memory of the translation. For the translation itself Philo writes some three centuries after the event and depends on the tradition also found in the [[source:letter-of-aristeas]]. For the festival he is a contemporary witness.
