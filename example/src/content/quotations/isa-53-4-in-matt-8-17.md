Matthew tells how [[person:jesus]] heals the mother of Peter's wife of a fever, and how that evening he casts out spirits with a word and heals all the sick who are brought to him. He says that this was to fulfil what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet [[bible:Matt 8:14-17]]. The line comes from the passage about the servant who is despised and who suffers [[bible:Isa 53:3-5]].

Matthew applies the line to illnesses that are taken away. In Isaiah the servant bears the sickness himself and is thought to be struck by God, and the next verse says that he was "pierced for our transgressions" [[bible:Isa 53:4-5]]. The ancient Greek translation already reads the line of sin: "He bears our sins, and is pained for us" [[cite:lxx]]. 1 Peter uses the same chapter in that sense, of Christ bearing sins in his body on the tree [[bible:1 Pet 2:24]]. Matthew's wording, with infirmities and diseases, is not that of the Greek translation and is closer to the Hebrew text as modern translations render it [[bible:Isa 53:4; Matt 8:17]].

Who the servant is has been read in more than one way. The readings are set out under [[event:prophecies-of-return|the prophecy of the suffering servant]].
