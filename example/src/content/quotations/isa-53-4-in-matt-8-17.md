Matthew tells how [[person:jesus]], at Capernaum, heals the mother of Peter's wife of a fever, and how that evening he casts out spirits with a word and heals all who are sick. He says that this was to fulfil what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet [[bible:Matt 8:5, 14-17]]. The line comes from the passage about the servant who is despised and who suffers [[bible:Isa 52:13; 53:3-5]].

In Matthew the line follows cures: the fever leaves the woman, and the sick are healed [[bible:Matt 8:15-16]]. In Isaiah the one who has borne the sickness is himself a man of suffering and is thought to be struck by God, and the next verse says that his wounds were for the transgressions of others [[bible:Isa 53:3-5]]. The ancient Greek translation already reads the line of sin: "He bears our sins, and is pained for us" [[cite:lxx]]. 1 Peter uses the same chapter in that sense, of Christ bearing sins in his body [[bible:1 Pet 2:24]]. Matthew's wording, with infirmities and diseases, is not that of the Greek translation and is closer to the Hebrew text as modern translations render it [[bible:Isa 53:4; Matt 8:17]]. Those translations differ in their words: the World English Bible has "sickness" and "suffering" in Isaiah, the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, "infirmities" and "diseases" [[bible:Isa 53:4]].

Who the servant is has been read in more than one way. The readings are set out under [[event:prophecies-of-return|the prophecy of the suffering servant]].
