Matthew introduces John the Baptist preaching in the wilderness of Judea and says: "this is he who was spoken of by Isaiah the prophet" [[bible:Matt 3:1-3]]. He does not say here that anything was fulfilled [[bible:Matt 3:3]]. Mark and Luke apply the same verse to John the Baptist, and in John he says it of himself [[bible:Mark 1:2-4; Luke 3:2-6; John 1:23]].

In Isaiah 40 the voice is not described. It calls for the way of the Lord to be prepared in the wilderness and for a highway in the desert for God: valleys are to be raised and hills made low, and the glory of the Lord will be revealed [[bible:Isa 40:3-5]]. The chapter opens with comfort for [[place:jerusalem]], and goes on to tell the cities of Judah that their God is coming [[bible:Isa 40:1-2, 9-10]].

The two forms divide the sentence differently. In Isaiah, as the World English Bible and the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition render the Hebrew, the wilderness is where the way is to be prepared [[bible:Isa 40:3]]. In Matthew it is where the voice cries, and it is where he has just placed John [[bible:Matt 3:1, 3]]. The ancient Greek translation already reads "The voice of one crying in the wilderness" [[cite:lxx]].
