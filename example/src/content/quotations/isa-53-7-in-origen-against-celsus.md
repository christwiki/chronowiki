Celsus had reproached Jesus with his sufferings: he received no help from the Father and could not help himself. Origen answers that the sufferings "were the subject of prophecy, along with the cause of them", and writes out the passage from "Lo, my Servant shall have understanding" to "because of the iniquities of My people was He led unto death" [[cite:origen]]. His wording follows the ancient Greek translation, in which the servant "bears our sins" [[cite:origen]].

In Isaiah the passage belongs to the chapters that speak to the exiles in [[place:babylon]], and it does not name the servant [[bible:Isa 52:13-53:8]]. Elsewhere in those chapters the servant is Israel by name [[bible:Isa 41:8-9]].

Origen himself records the other reading. In a disputation with Jews "who were reckoned wise men", his opponent said that the predictions "bore reference to the whole people, regarded as one individual", in dispersion and suffering. Origen replied with the line that the servant was led to death "because of the iniquities of My people", and asked how the people could be one person and the servant another [[cite:dispute]].
