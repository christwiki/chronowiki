Origen says that Celsus reproached Jesus with his sufferings: he received no help from the Father, or could not help himself. Origen answers that the sufferings "were the subject of prophecy, along with the cause of them", and writes out the passage from "Lo, my Servant shall have understanding" to "because of the iniquities of My people was He led unto death" [[cite:origen]]. His wording follows the ancient Greek translation, in which the servant "bears our sins" [[cite:origen]] [[cite:lxx]].

A few chapters earlier the book of Isaiah tells its hearers to leave [[place:babylon]] [[bible:Isa 48:20]]. The passage itself does not name the servant [[bible:Isa 52:13-53:8]]. Elsewhere in those chapters the servant is Israel by name, and in another place has a mission to Israel [[bible:Isa 41:8-9]] [[bible:Isa 49:5-6]].

Origen himself records another reading. In a disputation with Jews "who were reckoned wise men", his opponent said that the predictions "bore reference to the whole people, regarded as one individual", in dispersion and suffering. Origen replied with the line that the servant was led to death "because of the iniquities of My people", and asked how the servant could die for the sins of the people unless he were someone other than the people [[cite:dispute]].
