After telling how [[person:herod-the-great|Herod]] has the boys of Bethlehem killed, Matthew says that what was spoken by [[person:jeremiah]] the prophet was fulfilled, and quotes the lament of Rachel [[bible:Matt 2:16-18]]. Here he names the prophet. He writes "was fulfilled", where his two earlier fulfilment sayings have "that it might be fulfilled" [[bible:Matt 1:22; 2:15, 17]].

In Jeremiah the verse belongs to a promise of return. Rachel weeps for children who "are no more", and the answer follows directly: she is to keep her voice from weeping, because they will come back from the land of the enemy to their own territory [[bible:Jer 31:15-17]]. In Genesis Rachel is the mother of Joseph and Benjamin [[bible:Gen 35:24]], and the next lines of Jeremiah speak of Ephraim grieving [[bible:Jer 31:18-20]]. Jeremiah 40 names a Ramah as the place where the captain of the guard lets the prophet go, after he had been taken in chains among the captives of [[place:jerusalem]] and Judah who were being carried to [[place:babylon]] [[bible:Jer 40:1]].

Genesis puts Rachel's grave on the way to Ephrath, "also called Bethlehem", the town of Matthew's story [[bible:Gen 35:19; Matt 2:16]]. 1 Samuel places her tomb in Benjamin, at Zelzah [[bible:1 Sam 10:2]].
