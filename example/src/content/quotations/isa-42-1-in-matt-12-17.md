Matthew tells how the Pharisees plan to destroy [[person:jesus]], and how he withdraws, heals the crowds that follow him and orders them not to make him known. He says that this was to fulfil what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet [[bible:Matt 12:14-17]]. The quotation runs to four verses and ends with the nations hoping in the servant's name [[bible:Matt 12:18-21]].

Its wording is not that of the Hebrew text or of the ancient Greek translation throughout. Where Isaiah has "my chosen, in whom my soul delights", Matthew has "my beloved in whom my soul is well pleased" [[bible:Isa 42:1; Matt 12:18]]. The voice at the baptism and at the transfiguration speaks in Matthew of "my beloved Son", with whom it is well pleased [[bible:Matt 3:17; 17:5]]. Matthew's last line, "In his name, the nations will hope", is close to the ancient Greek translation, "in his name shall the Gentiles trust"; the Hebrew, as the World English Bible renders it, has the islands waiting for his law [[cite:lxx]] [[bible:Isa 42:4]].

The Hebrew text, as modern translations render it, does not say in these verses who the servant is [[bible:Isa 42:1-4]]. In the chapter before, the servant is Israel by name [[bible:Isa 41:8-9]], and the Greek translation has the names in this verse: "Jacob is my servant, I will help him: Israel is my chosen" [[cite:lxx]]. Matthew's quotation has neither name [[bible:Matt 12:18]].
